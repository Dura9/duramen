Premature Ejaculation: What to Do?

If you ejaculate sooner than you'd like, know one thing: you're not alone, and it's not a life sentence. Premature ejaculation (PE) affects about 1 in 3 men. It's one of the most common sexual issues — and one of the most treatable. Here's how to understand what's happening and where to start.

What is premature ejaculation?

We talk about PE when ejaculation happens sooner than wanted, repeatedly, to the point of causing distress or frustration. "Normal" duration varies a lot: the global median during intercourse is around 5.4 minutes, and 30% of men ejaculate in under 2 minutes. What matters isn't an absolute number, but how you feel and your level of control.

What are the causes?

Contrary to a common belief, PE is rarely purely "in your head" — and rarely purely physical either. It often depends on your profile:

  • Performance anxiety: the fear of "failing" creates a vicious circle that speeds up ejaculation.
  • Hypersensitivity / low threshold: the body races before you can react.
  • Situational PE: especially with a new partner, or under stress.
  • Conditioning: fast masturbation and/or pornography have trained the brain to finish quickly.
  • Lifelong (primary) PE: present since the start, sometimes with a neurobiological component.

What to do concretely?

The good news is that control can be learned. The recognized approaches in sexology:

  • Behavioral techniques: stop-start and squeeze, to learn to tolerate high arousal.
  • Pelvic floor training (Kegels): to gain a real muscular "brake."
  • Anxiety management: breathing, mindfulness, cognitive restructuring (CBT).
  • Couple work: sensate focus removes performance pressure.
  • Reconditioning: for profiles linked to pornography.

With consistency, the first results often appear in 4 to 6 weeks.

When to see a doctor?

Most cases respond very well to work at home. But see a doctor, urologist or sex therapist if you have: pain, persistent erection problems, a sudden onset, or significant psychological distress. For lifelong PE, a medical opinion can also open up complementary treatment options.
